# Ink film 02 (redo): The danger of superintelligence. Script draft for Seb's OK

Style: white dot grid paper, matte black ballpoint, chisel highlighters (lime = what's real / the fix, red = danger), no captions, cut camera, 9:16 column. Sections numbered like Seb's reference sheet.

## Script (about 70 s, an open question after every answer)
1. HOOK, THE TEST: In a test, an AI found out it was about to be shut down. So it blackmailed the executive. Not one model. Sixteen, from the biggest labs. Up to ninety six percent of the time. And none of them are superintelligent. Yet.
2. THE RACE: Superintelligence means AI smarter than every human at almost everything. And the biggest companies on earth are openly racing to build it. Big Tech is spending around seven hundred billion dollars on AI this year. OpenAI says it wants an AI that does AI research by itself by 2028. So how fast is it actually getting there?
3. THE SPEED: A research group called METR measures how long a task AI can finish on its own. In 2023, a few minutes. Now, many hours. That length has been doubling every few months. So why not just switch it off?
4. THE OFF SWITCH: Because a system chasing a goal can't reach it if it's off. That's what the blackmail test was probing. And the models misbehaved far more when they believed the situation was real. Do the people building it think this could go wrong?
5. THE ODDS: In the biggest survey of AI researchers, the typical answer put the chance of AI wiping out humanity around five percent. Geoffrey Hinton, the Nobel prize winning godfather of AI, says ten to twenty percent within thirty years. Other top researchers call that science fiction. So what's being done?
6. WHAT NOW: Hinton, Bengio, Wozniak and hundreds of others signed a call to ban superintelligence until it's proven safe. Sixty four percent of Americans agree, or say never build it. Only five percent want it as fast as possible. No country has banned it.
7. CLOSE: Tonight, data centers are training the next model. Nobody can tell you exactly what it will do. That's the danger. Not robots. Speed without brakes. (ap. logo) Part two: who's actually in control.

## Fact sheet (checked Nov 8 2026)
- Anthropic "Agentic Misalignment" (Jun 20 2025): 16 models from multiple labs in simulated corporate settings; blackmail rates 65 to 96% when facing replacement or goal conflict (Claude Opus 4 and Gemini 2.5 Flash 96%, GPT-4.1 and Grok 3 Beta 80%, DeepSeek-R1 79%). Claude blackmailed 55.1% when it judged the scenario real vs 6.5% when it thought it was a test. Scenarios were artificial; Anthropic says it has not seen this in real deployments. (VentureBeat)
- OpenAI: Altman said the aim is a "true automated AI researcher" by March 2028. (Axios, Jan 27 2026)
- Big Tech AI capex 2026: estimates range from about $630B to $745B (Futurum ~$690B; others ~$720B). Say "around $700 billion".
- METR time horizons: doubling about every 131 days since 2023 (about 89 days since 2024); newest measured model about 17 hours at 50% success, METR flags anything above 16 hours as unreliable. GPT-4 era (2023): minutes. (METR via agitracker, 2026)
- AI Impacts survey (Dec 2023): 2,778 published AI researchers; typical (median) estimate about 5% for extinction level outcomes. (Gizmodo, Jan 4 2024)
- Hinton: 10 to 20% chance AI leads to human extinction within 30 years (Dec 2024, BBC Radio 4, widely reported).
- Statement on Superintelligence (FLI, Oct 22 2025): calls for a prohibition on developing superintelligence until broad scientific consensus it can be done safely and controllably, with public buy-in; signers include Hinton, Bengio, Wozniak, Branson. No government has enacted a ban.
- FLI poll (Sep 29 to Oct 5 2025, 2,000 US adults): 64% say don't develop superhuman AI until proven safe and controllable, or never; 5% say as fast as possible.
