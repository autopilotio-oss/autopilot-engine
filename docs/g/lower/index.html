Lower your internet, phone and insurance bills

Most people overpay on bills they never shop around. Claude reads your bills, finds better prices and writes what to say when you call.

TIMEAbout 30 minutes
YOU NEEDYour latest internet, phone and insurance bills or policy pages, and Claude
COSTFree
Providers usually save their best prices for new customers, and insurance prices drift up at renewal. One focused hour of comparing and asking can lower a bill you pay every month.

Step by step

1

Gather your bills

Download your latest internet, phone and any insurance bills (car, renters, home). For insurance, get the declarations page: it lists your coverages, limits and price. Black out account and policy numbers before you upload.

2

Have Claude read every bill

Upload them to one Claude chat and paste this:

I uploaded my latest bills and insurance pages. For each one make a table: provider, what I get (speed, data, coverages and limits), monthly price, fees and add ons, promo end dates, and anything I might be paying for twice or not using. Flag every fee you can't explain. Then rank them by how much I could likely save, and tell me what you'd need from me to compare.
3

Compare internet plans with the broadband label

Internet providers have to publish a broadband label for each plan they sell, sometimes behind a link or icon on the plan page: the monthly price, any intro rate, fees, speeds and data caps. Pull the label from 2 or 3 providers at your address and give them to Claude:

Here are broadband labels for plans at my address: [paste]. Compare them to what I pay now, including fees and the price after any promo ends. Which is cheapest over 12 and 24 months for the speed I actually need? I use the internet for: [streaming, work calls, gaming, number of people].
FCC: broadband labels ↗
4

Get 3 insurance quotes for the same coverage

State regulators suggest reviewing your policy once a year and comparing quotes. Ask for quotes with the same coverages and limits so you compare like for like, and ask each company which discounts you qualify for.

Here are my current policy and 3 quotes: [paste]. Line them up side by side: same coverages and limits? deductible? price per year after discounts? Flag anything weaker than my current policy. Then list the discounts I should ask my current insurer about.
NAIC: comparing auto insurance quotes ↗Find your state insurance department ↗
5

Call and ask, with a script

With a better offer in hand, call your current provider and ask for the retention or loyalty department. Have Claude write the script and practice once:

Write a short phone script for calling [provider] about my [service]. I pay $[amount]. A competitor offers [offer]. Ask for a lower price or a promotion, ask them to remove [fees], and tell me what to say if they say no. Keep it polite and under 30 seconds per answer. Then play the agent and push back so I can practice.
6

Ask your bank to waive a fee

Banks can refund a fee as a courtesy, especially if it's your first in a while. Some banks have cut or dropped overdraft fees entirely, so it's also worth checking if a different account would cost you less.

Write a short, polite message to [bank] asking them to refund a $[amount] [overdraft / monthly / late] fee charged on [date]. I've been a customer for [time] and [reason it happened]. Ask what I can change so it doesn't happen again.
7

If money is tight, check Lifeline

Lifeline is a federal program that gives eligible lower income households up to $9.25 a month off one phone or internet service (more on Tribal lands). One discount per household. You apply through the National Verifier or a participating provider.

Watch out

  • Never switch insurance until the new policy is active. A gap in coverage can cost you more later.
  • Cheaper isn't better if coverage is weaker. Compare limits and deductibles, not just price.
  • Read the end date of any promo price and put it in your calendar.
  • Claude drafts the scripts and messages. You make the calls and decide what to accept.
