Get your money back

Canceled flights, double charges, things that never arrived. You often have a legal right to a refund. Here's how to claim it with Claude.

Companies don't always refund you automatically, even when they should. A clear, specific request that names your rights gets results far more often than an angry phone call.

Step by step

1

Find your refunds

Have Claude scan your recent statements and emails for money you may be owed:

I uploaded my last 3 months of card statements. Find anything that could be refundable: duplicate charges, charges for things I canceled, orders that may not have arrived, canceled or changed flights, hotel or rental fees, price increases, and fees I didn't agree to. Make a table: merchant, date, amount, why it might be refundable, and what proof I should find. Ask me before assuming.
2

Flights: know the rule

Under U.S. Department of Transportation rules, if an airline cancels your flight or makes a significant change and you don't accept the alternative, you're entitled to a cash refund, including taxes and fees, not just a voucher. Significant changes include departing or arriving 3+ hours off for domestic flights or 6+ hours for international. Refunds are due within 7 business days for card payments.

3

Write a request that gets approved

Paste your booking or receipt details and use this:

Write a refund request to [company]. Order or booking number: [number]. What happened: [facts and dates]. What I want: a refund of $[amount] to my original payment method. If it applies, mention my rights [for flights: the DOT refund rule for canceled or significantly changed flights / for cards: my right to dispute billing errors]. Keep it short, specific and polite, list what I'm attaching, and give them 7 days to respond.
4

Escalate if they say no

If the company refuses or ignores you, file a complaint. For airlines, file with DOT. For card charges, dispute with your card issuer within 60 days of the statement. Have Claude write it:

Write a dispute for my card issuer. Charge: [merchant, date, amount]. What went wrong: [facts]. What I did to fix it with the merchant: [dates and responses]. Ask them to remove the charge as a billing error, and list the proof I'm attaching.

5

Keep a paper trail

Save every email, screenshot and reference number. Claude can turn your notes into a timeline in seconds, and a clean timeline is what wins disputes.

Watch out

  • Claude helps you write the request. The company, the DOT or your card issuer decides the outcome.
  • Only dispute charges that are actually wrong. A dispute isn't a way to get out of a purchase you agreed to.
  • Debit cards have different and often weaker protections than credit cards. Act fast.
