Get your security deposit back

Your landlord kept part of your deposit? Here's how to check every charge and ask for the money back in writing, with Claude doing the sorting and the drafting.

TIMEAbout 30 minutes
YOU NEEDYour lease, move in and move out photos, the landlord's list of deductions, and Claude
COSTFree
Landlords can usually deduct for unpaid rent and real damage, but generally not for normal wear and tear, and most states set a deadline to return the deposit or send an itemized list. In New York, thousands of renters have filed complaints about missing deposits. A clear, polite letter with your photos is often enough to get money back.

Step by step

1

Find your state's deadline

Search your state's official site (attorney general or state courts) for 'security deposit'. Write down how many days the landlord has after you move out, and what they must send you. For example, California gives landlords 21 days and New York gives 14. Some cities have extra rules.

2

Gather your proof

Put these in one folder: your lease, the move in checklist, move in and move out photos with dates, the landlord's itemized list, any receipts they sent, and the date you moved out and returned the keys.

3

Have Claude sort every deduction

Upload everything to one Claude chat and paste this:

I uploaded my lease, my move in and move out photos and my landlord's list of deductions from my security deposit. Make a table with one row per deduction: item, amount, and your call: Fair, Normal wear and tear, Pre-existing, No proof, or Too high, with the photo or lease clause that supports it. Normal wear and tear is deterioration from ordinary use, like worn carpet or faded paint. If you can't tell from the photos, say so. Don't guess any law or deadline. Then total what I could fairly dispute.
4

Check the timing

Compare your move out date with the date the list arrived. If it came after your state's deadline, or never came, note it. Paste this:

My state is [state]. The official deadline is [number] days and the landlord must send [what your state requires]. I moved out on [date] and got the list on [date or never]. Was it on time? Show your math.
5

Write the demand letter

Ask Claude for a short, polite, firm letter. Read it, fix anything that isn't exactly true, and send it yourself.

Write a short demand letter to my landlord. Include my move out date, the deposit amount, each disputed charge with one line on why, the total I'm asking for, my forwarding address and a reply date 14 days from today. No threats, no legal jargon, nothing I didn't tell you.
6

Send it so you can prove it

Email it and also send it by certified mail with tracking. Keep copies of everything. If there's no answer by your date, look up small claims court on your state's courts site. Many deposit cases are handled there without a lawyer, and local legal aid can help for free.

Watch out

  • Deadlines, rules and penalties are different in every state and some cities. Check your state's official page before you send anything.
  • This guide is education, not legal advice. For a large amount or a complicated case, contact local legal aid or a tenant rights group.
  • Never add damage, dates or receipts that aren't real. Your photos and documents are what make your case.
