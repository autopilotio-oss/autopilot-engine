Make TikToks with AI that plans every edit and gets your posts scheduled

Claude finds what works in your niche, writes the scripts, plans every cut and caption, and can schedule the posts if you connect a scheduler. You film and cut the clips.

TIMEAbout 1 hour for a full week of videos
YOU NEEDA phone, CapCut (free), and Claude
COSTFree
Most of the work in a TikTok isn't filming. It's the hook, the script, the cuts, the captions and posting at the right time. Claude does the thinking and writing, CapCut does the cutting, and a scheduler posts on time.

Step by step

1

Find what's working in your niche

Open TikTok, search your niche, and write down the first line (the hook) and topic of 10 recent videos (last 30 days) from accounts close to your size that got far more views than they have followers. Huge accounts teach the wrong lessons. Paste them into Claude:

Here are the hooks and topics of 10 high view TikToks from the last 30 days in my niche [niche]: [paste]. Find the patterns: which hook styles repeat, which topics, how fast they get to the point, and what makes people comment. Then give me 10 video ideas for my account that use those patterns but say something new. My account is about [what you do] for [who].
2

Write a week of scripts in one go

Pick your best 7 ideas and have Claude write them so they're ready to film:

Write 7 TikTok scripts from these ideas: [paste]. Each one: a hook that works in the first 2 seconds, 3 short beats, and a clear ending (follow, comment a word, or a question). Under 30 seconds when spoken. Add the on screen text for each beat and one line on what to show. Write how I talk: [describe your voice]. No hype words.
3

Turn each script into an edit plan

This is the part that makes AI your editor. Claude can't cut the video itself, but it tells you exactly where every cut, caption and sound goes:

Turn this script into an edit plan for CapCut: [paste script]. Give me a shot list (what to film or which clip), where each cut goes in seconds, the text on screen and when it appears, where to zoom or add B roll, and the type of sound to use. Keep the first 2 seconds as strong as possible.
4

Edit fast in CapCut

Film the shots, drop them in CapCut, cut where the plan says, and use Auto captions so the words appear on screen. Captions help people who watch in noisy places or with the sound off, and they make your video easier to find in TikTok search. For anything you sell, only use sounds from TikTok's or CapCut's commercial library. If you ever use AI to make realistic people, voices or scenes, turn on TikTok's AI generated content label.

CapCut ↗TikTok: AI generated content ↗
5

Get the caption, hashtags and cover

Put the words people would search for in your caption and on screen text, and say them out loud in the first few seconds: TikTok uses all three to show your video in search. Paste the final script and let Claude write the post:

Write the TikTok post for this video: [paste script]. Give me a caption under 150 characters that adds to the video and includes the words people would search for, 4 relevant hashtags (no generic ones like #fyp), and 3 options for the cover text, max 5 words each.
6

Get it posted on time

With a Creator or Business account, TikTok Studio in a browser lets you schedule videos up to 10 days ahead. A scheduler like Metricool lets you schedule from your phone, and if you connect Metricool to Claude, Claude can fill in the caption and time and schedule it: you upload the video, it does the rest. Metricool's free plan covers about 20 scheduled posts a month, so daily posting needs a paid plan or TikTok Studio. Schedulers usually can't add TikTok Shop product links or trending sounds, so post shoppable videos from the TikTok app.

TikTok Studio ↗Metricool ↗
7

Review every week and double down

Once a week, paste your numbers so Claude tells you what to make more of:

Here are my TikTok numbers from this week: [for each video: topic, hook, views, average watch time or % watched, likes, comments, shares, follows]. Tell me which hooks and topics are working, what to stop, and plan next week's 7 videos doubling down on the winner with one new test.

Watch out

  • Claude plans and writes. Watch every video yourself before it goes out.
  • Label realistic AI generated content on TikTok. Never use AI to show a real person doing or saying something they didn't.
  • Don't repost other creators' videos or use music you don't have rights to in ads.
