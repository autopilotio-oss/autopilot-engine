RULES: • Use these categories: housing, food, transport, subscriptions, shopping, fun, fees, other • Don’t skip small charges. They add up • Flag anything you can’t identify GIVE ME: a table with category, total spent, % of my spending, and my 3 biggest single charges.
2. Find subscriptionsYou are a financial advisor who charges $200 an hour and works with everyday people, not millionaires. TASK: Find every subscription and repeating charge in my statement. RULES: • Include yearly charges split into a monthly cost • Mark each one KEEP, CUT or CHECK, with a one line reason • Look for two services that do the same thing GIVE ME: a table of each charge, monthly cost and verdict, plus the total I’d save a year if I cut everything marked CUT.
3. Catch mistakesYou are a bank fraud analyst. TASK: Check my statement for anything that should not be there. RULES: • Look for double charges, hidden fees, price increases and free trials that started billing • Compare repeating charges month to month if you can • Don’t accuse. 4. Find the leaksYou are a financial advisor who charges $200 an hour and works with everyday people, not millionaires. RULES: • Rank them by how much I’d save in a year • Give one easy swap for each, not ‘just stop’ • Be honest, even if it stings GIVE ME: the 5 leaks, the yearly cost of each, the swap, and my total possible savings.

If you must use one, explain it • Go section by section • Tell me what kind of contract this is and who it favors GIVE ME: a short summary, then one plain sentence for each section.
2. What you payYou are a contract lawyer who charges $400 an hour and explains things in plain English. TASK: Find every dollar this contract makes me pay. RULES: • Include fees, deposits, penalties, automatic renewals and price increases • Quote the exact line for each one • Add up the total cost over the full term GIVE ME: a table of what I pay, when, and the worst case total.
3. Red flagsYou are a contract lawyer who charges $400 an hour and explains things in plain English. TASK: Find the red flags in this contract. RULES: • Look for one sided terms, hidden renewals, unfair penalties and vague wording • Rate each one: normal, watch out, or walk away • Compare it to what’s standard for this kind of contract GIVE ME: a ranked list of red flags with the exact wording and why each one matters.
4. Your way outYou are a contract lawyer who charges $400 an hour and explains things in plain English. TASK: Tell me every way I can get out of this contract. RULES: • Cover cancelling early, ending it on time, and what happens if they break it • Include notice periods and deadlines • Show what each exit costs me GIVE ME: my exit options from cheapest to most expensive, with the steps for each.
5. The penaltiesYou are a contract lawyer who charges $400 an hour and explains things in plain English. TASK: Show me what happens if something goes wrong. RULES: • Cover late payment, a missed payment, breaking a rule and a dispute • Quote the lines that apply • Tell me which penalty is the most dangerous GIVE ME: a simple if this, then that list for every penalty.
6. Ask for changesYou are a contract lawyer who charges $400 an hour and explains things in plain English. TASK: Find the 3 to 5 terms I should negotiate before signing. RULES: • Pick the ones that save me the most money or risk • Suggest fairer wording for each • Keep the tone friendly so the deal doesn’t die GIVE ME: the list of changes and a ready to send message asking for them.
7. Before you signYou are a contract lawyer who charges $400 an hour and explains things in plain English. TASK: Give me a final check before I sign. RULES: • List what’s missing that should be in here • Give me the questions to ask the other side • End with your honest verdict GIVE ME: 5 questions to ask, what’s missing, and a clear sign, negotiate or walk away.

Claude helps you understand it. For big contracts, still talk to a lawyer.

Prompt Claude like the 1%

1. Give it a jobAct as a [type of expert] with 20 years of experience who explains things simply. Why it works: Claude answers like the expert you name, not like a search engine.
2. Tell it your situationHere’s my situation: [who you are, what’s going on, what you’ve tried]. Why it works: the more it knows about you, the less generic the answer.
3. Say what you want backGive me the answer as [a table / 5 bullets / a step by step plan] in under [number] words. Why it works: you get something you can use right away.
4. Let it ask firstBefore you answer, ask me up to 5 questions so your answer fits me exactly. Why it works: it stops guessing and starts tailoring.
5. Show an exampleHere’s an example of what I like: [paste]. Match this style. Why it works: one example beats ten sentences of explaining.

7. Make it check itselfNow act as a tough critic. What’s weak or wrong in your answer? Fix it and give me the better version. Why it works: the second answer is almost always stronger.
